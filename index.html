PROJECT 01

Supporting the employee lifecycle from entry to exit

A 50-person financial services company · HR Operations Intern · Hiring, onboarding, employment records and exit

COVERED
ENTRY TO EXIT

The brief

The company was growing and its HR processes were scattered. My task was to give every stage of an employee's journey a clear process, a template and a record, so that nothing depended on memory or on one person.

Onboarding: an employee-facing checklist

I framed onboarding as a self-check task list for the new joiner, not as an HR process document, so people stay engaged from day one. Part A applies to everyone: introduction with HR, asset handover, system logins, biometric registration, documentation forms, receiving and then signing the appointment letter and service agreement, a 14-policy reading list, company reading, salary account opening and training videos. Part B is department specific, for Financial Research and Analysis and for Finance and Accounts.

A sequential form made the path enforceable: required checkboxes with branching stand in for locked sections, because the form tool cannot lock them.

One record per employee, linked to attendance and leave

The individual employee workbook holds one sheet per employee. Master attendance and leave sheets pull from those sheets with lookup formulas, so a change in one place updates everywhere.

Exit: a structured interview and clear separation rules

I built the exit interview form in Word and in Excel, so feedback is collected the same way every time and can feed attrition analysis. The absconding and notice period buyout policies were each written as a detailed reference document first, then reduced to a short policy employees can read.

Exit reasons and department-wise exits from this process were analysed in the attrition dashboard, so the interview data does not sit unused.

PROJECT 02

Keeping employee communication clear and consistent

Same 50-person organisation · HR Operations Intern · Guidelines, intranet and day-to-day employee relations

WRITTEN & BUILT
FOR DAILY USE

The brief

Messages travelled across founders, department heads and teams in different formats and tones. I wrote down the norms, built one place for company information, and handled the daily flow of queries so employees knew where to go and what to expect.

Four channels of work

Internal communication guidelines
A 2 to 3 page manual covering core principles, dos and don'ts, junior to senior and senior to junior conduct, email etiquette and Teams etiquette. English is the working language, seniors do not reprimand juniors in public, and Teams calls are kept for genuine emergencies.
Company intranet
A branded portal with announcements, leadership pages, an employee directory, an HR hub with policy tiles and an FAQ, plus a home page showing birthdays, work anniversaries and recognition.
Day-to-day employee relations
Handling employee queries, carrying messages between management and teams, vendor communication, and standard messages such as ID card photos, details forms, reimbursement requests and event invitations.
Stakeholder coordination
Working with founders, department heads, the HR manager, the accounts team and outside vendors, and preparing agendas and minutes for programmes and activities.

Listening to employees

I designed a questionnaire on employee perspectives on HR policies, with charts for each question, to practise turning employee opinion into findings. The analysis ran on sample responses, so it shows the method and not a company result.

The intranet is covered in detail in the HR Operations portfolio. Here it appears as a communication channel: it is where policies, news and recognition reach every employee in one place.

Rules that keep it fair

Employees need 3 months of tenure, matching probation. Managers nominate; there is no self-nomination. Nominations are scored out of 100 on an Excel form and final evaluation sits with senior management. Winners receive a certificate, public recognition and a gift.

Other benefits and development

Referral programme
Policy slides and an email template covering who can refer, candidate eligibility, limits, process and bonus structure.
Joining bonus
A 2 page policy drafted from a reference document, in plain employee-facing language.
Future Leadership Programme
An Individual Development Plan template, rebuilt as a clean two-page landscape form.
ESOP and leave
An ESOP governance framework within the performance system, and clear leave and WFH entitlements.
Recognition was linked to the performance system: an employee on an active improvement plan or with an unresolved disciplinary matter is not eligible for nomination.

PROJECT 04

Planning employee engagement that brings people together

Same 50-person organisation · HR Operations Intern · Community, creative, family and festive activities

PLANNED &
RUN WITH TEAMS

The brief

People worked in different departments and sometimes in different cities, with little time to know each other beyond their desks. I planned a mix of formats that fit a working day: giving back, creating together, sharing food, and inviting families in. Each event had an agenda, an invitation, a materials list and a reimbursement note, so the next team can repeat it.
